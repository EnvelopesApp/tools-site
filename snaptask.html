SnapTask

One shortcut. One task. Back to what you were doing.

Press Ctrl+Space and a small glass box appears over whatever is on screen. Type what needs doing, pick a date, choose the list or calendar it belongs in, and press Enter. SnapTask files it in Google Tasks or Google Calendar and disappears.

What it does

A capture box, not another app to live in.

Type it and forget it

The whole interaction is one keystroke, a sentence, and Enter. Nothing to file, sort, or tidy afterwards.

Tasks or calendar, your choice

Every capture shows exactly where it is going before you commit, and you can send this one somewhere else without changing your default.

Every list and calendar

Pick from the task lists and calendars your Google account actually has, rather than whichever one the app decided was primary.

Never invents a due date

If you do not set a date, the task has no date. SnapTask will offer one it found in text you pasted, but it will not guess on your behalf.

Starts with what you copied

Copy a ticket title or a line from an email first and the box opens pre-filled. Reading the clipboard needs no permission, and SnapTask never writes to it.

Times survive

Google Tasks discards the time of day. If you set one and file it as a task anyway, SnapTask keeps it in the notes rather than letting it vanish.

Permissions

SnapTask asks your Mac for nothing.

Most capture tools of this kind ask for Accessibility access so they can read your selected text, and Automation access so they can drive other apps. SnapTask does neither. You type the task yourself, which means there is nothing to read, nothing to automate, and no permission prompt.

It does not simulate keystrokes, and it never writes to your clipboard. The clipboard is read once, when the box opens, only to offer a starting point you can overwrite or delete.

Your Google connection uses the standard sign-in flow in your own browser. SnapTask never sees your password, and the token it receives is stored in the macOS Keychain rather than in a file. Nothing you capture is sent to Envelopes.

How it works

Four steps, about three seconds.

  1. Press Ctrl+Space. The box appears on the display your cursor is on, over whatever you were reading.
  2. Type the task. Or edit what SnapTask pre-filled from your clipboard.
  3. Pick a date, if it needs one. Today, tomorrow, a named weekday, or the calendar. Add a time and it becomes a calendar event.
  4. Press Enter. It appears in Google, and the box is gone.
